Learn the math
when it matters.

A staged route for economics, finance, and econometrics: build the computational foundation first, add rigorous analysis for theory, and learn specialized mathematics alongside the research that uses it.

01 / Principle

Do not study everything at once.

Mathematics is infrastructure, not a gate that must be completely crossed before economics begins. The right depth depends on the research question.

Applied empirical work

Master Stages 1–4.

Calculus, linear algebra, probability, statistics, and optimization cover most empirical finance, regression work, forecasting, and applied machine learning. Add later topics only when the model requires them.

Strong computation first
Theoretical econometrics

Study Stages 1–6 systematically.

Method development requires proof literacy, rigorous convergence, measurable random variables, conditional expectation, and asymptotic arguments. Real analysis and measure-theoretic probability are working tools here.

Foundations cannot be skipped
Macro & asset pricing

Learn Stages 4–7 with the models.

Bellman equations, recursive methods, Markov processes, martingales, and continuous-time models become easier through repeated use in macroeconomics and asset pricing.

Theory and mathematics together
Research rule

Foundation first. Specialization on demand.

Do not delay economics until every advanced mathematics course is finished. Build a reliable core, then return to deeper mathematics whenever a theorem, model, or proof makes it necessary.

Learn → apply → revisit
02 / Core sequence

Eight stages, each with a purpose.

The sequence moves from calculation to proof, from probability to econometric theory. Later stages build on the earlier ones but can overlap with field courses.

Stage 01Foundation

Calculus

Why learn itCalculus describes change, accumulation, and local approximation—the basic language of marginal decisions and continuous models.

Where it appearsMarginal utility and cost, comparative statics, compounding, duration, option sensitivities, growth models, likelihood derivatives, and continuous-time finance.

Study targetSingle- and multivariable differentiation, integration, sequences and series, partial derivatives, Taylor approximation, gradients, Jacobians, and Hessians.

Song Hao · Advanced Mathematics ↗
Stage 02Foundation

Linear Algebra

Why learn itEconomic and financial data are multivariate. Linear algebra turns many equations and variables into objects that can be analyzed and computed efficiently.

Where it appearsOLS, covariance matrices, portfolio optimization, factor models, principal components, state-space models, network models, and machine learning.

Study targetVectors, matrices, rank, linear systems, projections, quadratic forms, eigenvalues, positive definiteness, decompositions, and matrix differentiation.

Song Hao · Linear Algebra ↗
Stage 03Foundation

Probability & Mathematical Statistics

Why learn itMarkets and data are uncertain. Probability models uncertainty; statistics turns samples into estimates, tests, and forecasts.

Where it appearsRisk measurement, distributions of returns, regression, maximum likelihood, hypothesis testing, forecast evaluation, credit risk, and empirical research.

Study targetRandom variables, common distributions, joint and conditional probability, expectation, variance, laws of large numbers, central limit theorem, estimation, testing, and regression foundations.

Song Hao · Probability & Statistics ↗
Stage 04Core tool

Optimization & Dynamic Programming

Why learn itEconomics studies choices under constraints. Optimization converts preferences, technologies, and trading decisions into precise solutions.

Where it appearsConsumer and firm problems, portfolio choice, estimation, equilibrium, constrained finance problems, optimal control, recursive macroeconomics, and Bellman equations.

Study targetConvexity, first- and second-order conditions, constrained optimization, Lagrange multipliers, KKT conditions, envelope theorem, fixed points, Bellman recursion, and numerical solution methods.

Course guide coming soon
Stage 05Theory foundation

Real Analysis

Why learn itReal analysis explains why limits, continuity, optimization, and convergence arguments are valid. It replaces intuition with conditions and proof.

Where it appearsExistence and uniqueness of solutions, consistency proofs, uniform convergence, interchange of limits and integrals, fixed-point arguments, equilibrium theory, and rigorous asymptotics.

Study targetProof methods, sequences, limits, continuity, compactness, metric spaces, differentiation, Riemann integration, uniform convergence, and contraction mappings. Learn the core before attempting a full pure-mathematics sequence.

Recommended before theoretical econometrics
Stage 06Theory foundation

Measure Theory & Measure-Theoretic Probability

Why learn itMeasure theory is the rigorous language beneath modern probability. It defines random variables, expectations, conditioning, and convergence precisely enough for advanced statistics.

Where it appearsAsymptotic econometrics, conditional expectation, stochastic processes, martingales, semiparametric theory, continuous-time asset pricing, and stochastic calculus.

Study targetSigma-algebras, measures, measurable functions, Lebesgue integration, convergence theorems, modes of stochastic convergence, product measures, conditional expectation, and probability spaces.

Essential for method development; optional for routine empirical work
Stage 07Field tool

Stochastic Processes

Why learn itFinancial and economic variables evolve through time under uncertainty. Stochastic processes model both the uncertainty and the temporal structure.

Where it appearsTime-series models, Markov decisions, asset prices, volatility, credit events, term structures, derivatives, macroeconomic states, and risk management.

Study targetMarkov chains, stationary processes, martingales, Brownian motion, Poisson processes, stopping times, and an introduction to stochastic differential equations. Learn stochastic calculus only when the research requires it.

Best learned alongside macro, time series, or asset pricing
Stage 08Research integration

Econometric Theory

Why learn itThis stage turns the earlier mathematics into research methods. It explains identification, estimation, inference, and the conditions under which empirical conclusions are reliable.

Where it appearsOLS and maximum likelihood theory, GMM, panel data, time series, causal inference, semiparametric methods, financial econometrics, and new estimator development.

Study targetIdentification, consistency, asymptotic normality, extremum estimators, M- and Z-estimation, GMM, delta methods, stochastic orders, bootstrap logic, and empirical-process ideas when needed.

The destination for a methods-oriented researcher
03 / Choose a destination

One foundation. Different research routes.

Financial econometrics and derivatives pricing share probability, analysis, and optimization, but they diverge after the foundation. Choose the branch that matches the question you want to answer.

Primary route · Methods

Financial & Theoretical Econometrics

Why follow it: this route is for developing estimators, studying identification and inference, explaining financial data, and building methods whose reliability can be proved.

Probability → asymptotics → econometrics
Specialized route · Pricing

Derivatives & Mathematical Finance

Why follow it: this route is for pricing contingent claims and modelling asset prices, volatility, credit events, and interest rates in discrete or continuous time.

Martingales → Itô calculus → SDEs
Financial econometrics · main routeCalculus → Linear Algebra → Probability & Statistics → Optimization → Real Analysis → Measure-Theoretic Probability → Mathematical Statistics & Asymptotic Theory → Econometric Theory → Time Series & Financial Econometrics → Empirical Processes and Semiparametric Methods, when needed
Derivatives pricing · optional branchProbability → Discrete-Time Asset Pricing → Martingales → Brownian Motion → Itô Calculus → Stochastic Differential Equations → Continuous-Time Asset Pricing → Numerical Methods and Model Calibration

A practical stopping ruleFor standard empirical finance, stop after the econometric tools needed by the project. For vanilla derivatives, Shreve I–II plus numerical methods form a strong base. Continue to advanced stochastic analysis or functional analysis only when the research problem demands it.
04 / Optional extensions

Useful later, not prerequisites now.

These subjects matter in particular research areas, but they should not displace the core sequence unless a model or paper creates a clear need.

Research extension

Functional Analysis

Why learn it: it studies functions as points in spaces and operators acting on them. It becomes useful in nonparametric and semiparametric econometrics, inverse problems, infinite-dimensional optimization, term-structure models, and advanced asset pricing.

Learn when functions become parameters
Optional extension

Abstract Algebra

Why learn it: it develops structural reasoning through groups, rings, and fields. It is intellectually valuable but rarely the main bottleneck in economics or finance, so it has lower priority than analysis, probability, optimization, and linear algebra.

Study only for a specific theoretical need
