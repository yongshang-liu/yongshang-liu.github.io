WEEK OF 2026-11-06 · VERIFIED THEORY SELECTION

High-Dimensional Learning, Random Matrices & Equilibrium Asset Pricing

Complex Capital Markets

Sergei Glebkin, Bryan Kelly, Semyon Malamud, Matias Palmunen, Mohammad Pourmohammadi · CEPR DP22144 · Published 2026-11-05

Research question: Can many individually weak predictors sustain return predictability and an equity premium when investors endogenously choose costly model sizes and learn from equilibrium prices?

Model and assumptions (verified from full text): A one-period rational-expectations asset market with CARA Bayesian investors, noise trading, a finite strong-signal block, and N Gaussian weak signals. The payoff is d = b′s + β′S + ε, with weak coefficients of order N−1/2, Gaussian coefficient priors and an inverse-Wishart covariance prior. The high-dimensional regime has N,T → ∞ and N/T → c > 0; investors pay to acquire nested signal sets.

Core theoretical results: Theorem 1 establishes a unique complex rational expectations equilibrium for a fixed informed fraction and linear limiting demands/prices. Theorem 5 characterizes an essentially unique linear equilibrium with endogenous model-scale choices under its cost restrictions; larger and smaller models can coexist because prices reveal information from larger models. The paper also relates the limiting economy to regularized finite economies.

Proof strategy: Random-matrix limits simplify high-dimensional Bayesian posterior uncertainty into a limiting Gaussian forecast representation. Conditional CARA demands, inference from prices, and market clearing yield equilibrium coefficients; indifference conditions and residual-risk monotonicity characterize the endogenous scale distribution. See the paper's Appendices C, F and L for exact proofs.

Innovation and numerical evidence: Integrates high-dimensional parameter estimation, inference from prices and endogenous computational choice in a tractable equilibrium. Calibration studies computing costs, heterogeneous model scale, Sharpe-ratio dispersion and a persistent equity premium. This is theoretical asset pricing rather than a new frequentist estimator.

Limitations: Main analysis relies on Gaussian signals/priors, CARA utility, a one-period environment and the linear equilibrium class; extensions to nonlinear prices, heterogeneous preferences and adaptive acquisition are not established by the principal results.

Research extensions (editorial suggestions, not author claims): Explore non-Gaussian heavy-tailed random-matrix limits; derive finite-sample rates for the equilibrium approximation; test identification of information-acquisition cost parameters; study conditional factor structures in a multi-asset dynamic extension. Possible journal fit (speculative): Journal of Finance, Journal of Financial Economics, Review of Financial Studies, or theoretical finance/economics journals depending on revisions.

WEEK OF 2026-10-30 · READING SELECTION

Conditional Factors, Spectral Structure & High-Dimensional Inference

Nonlinear Factor Models and the Cross-Section of Asset Returns

Georgy Chabakauri · CEPR DP22081 · 2026-10-24

Research question: How do nonlinear latent factors relate to PCA components and covariance eigenvalues? The study derives analytical relationships and examines how small changes in factor exposures may alter the number of statistically selected components.

Why read: Nonlinear factor structure, eigenvalue spectra, and the distinction between economic and statistical factors.

The Hidden Sharpe Ratio

Mikhail Chernov, Bryan Kelly, Semyon Malamud, Johannes Schwab · CEPR DP22099 · 2026-10-27

Research question: How can the maximum attainable Sharpe ratio be statistically inferred in high dimensions without first estimating an optimal portfolio? The work studies bounds, asymptotic inference, and the gap between regularized portfolios and the theoretical maximum.

Why read: High-dimensional financial inference, covariance inversion, regularization bias, and asymptotic confidence intervals.

OCTOBER 2026 · PREVIOUS READING PICK (ARCHIVED 2026-10-30)

Infinite-Dimensional Factor Structure in Conditional Asset Pricing

Infinite Factor Structure in Conditional Asset Pricing

Mikhail Chernov, Bryan T. Kelly, Semyon Malamud, Johannes Schwab · NBER Working Paper 35910 · Issued October 2026

Research question: Does a finite conditional factor structure remain finite when returns are viewed unconditionally across economic states?

Main theoretical result: The paper shows that, generically, a conditionally finite-factor model can exhibit an unconditional infinite-factor structure: infinitely many covariance eigenvalues diverge as the asset universe grows. This phenomenon also applies to characteristic-managed portfolios and to stocks with persistently heterogeneous loadings. Under the absence of asymptotic arbitrage in conditional expected returns, the maximal Sharpe ratio remains finite. Generically, infinitely many components are priced, so a fixed finite number of principal components need not span expected returns.

Why read: A fundamental challenge to finite-factor PCA approximations, with implications for spectral theory, factor identification, covariance estimation, and asset-pricing inference.

Possible extension (reading suggestion, not a claim of the paper): Investigate statistical tests for conditional versus unconditional factor dimensionality, and characterize high-dimensional eigenvalue behavior under state-dependent loadings. Check the paper and appendix for existing results before treating this as an open problem.
